BC Mineral Claims Search: Find Claims by Owner, Number or Map

Search British Columbia mineral, placer and coal titles by registered owner, tenure number or area below and see them on a map with owner, good-to date, area and title type. It is free, needs no account, and queries the province's public tenure layer, published by DataBC from Mineral Titles Online (MTO) records, each time you search.

Free · no login · public registry data (live DataBC tenure query, verify in MTO)

Look up a BC claim by tenure number

Choose Claim number(s) and enter the tenure number MTO shows, for example 1044501; an older staking tag number works too. You can paste a list from a claim schedule — up to 100 numbers separated by spaces, commas or new lines — and you are told which were found and which were not. If a single number is not found, you get the titles whose numbers start with what you typed, labelled as near matches rather than presented as yours.

When a number is not found

Check the number against your source, then look it up in Mineral Titles Online — the search links to it. MTO is the registry of record, and the public layer we search is a copy of it.

Look up BC claims by owner (company)

Choose Company and type the registered owner name, for example Teck. Every word must match, in any order, and if the full legal name finds nothing the search retries with the most distinctive words and tells you it did. If several owners match, pick the right one; their titles are then grouped by area with the claim count, total hectares and the range of good-to dates.

MTO records the legal owner on title, which is often a subsidiary or a joint-venture partner rather than the parent company. An empty name match is not evidence that the ground is unheld; try one distinctive word or a tenure number. The company claim-search guide covers mapping a whole land position.

Find BC claims in an area on the map

Choose Area and enter a latitude and longitude, for example 56.6, -130.4 in the Golden Triangle. The map opens on that point and loads every title within 25 km, each owner in its own colour; you can change the radius to 10, 50 or 100 km. Leave the box empty and the map opens on British Columbia: click the point you want, then Load Claims. There is no map-sheet search for BC.

For work history, transfers and anything that governs a transaction, open the title in Mineral Titles Online.

Check a claim in Mineral Titles Online (MTO), the official registry

  1. Open Mineral Titles Online at mtonline.gov.bc.ca.
  2. Use its title search to look up the tenure number, or find the claim in its Map Viewer.
  3. Open the title to read its current owner, status and good-to date.
  4. Treat MTO as the answer wherever it differs from a map: it is the registry of record.
Verify in BC Mineral Titles Online (MTO)
Always verify current ownership, status, expiry, good-to dates, and boundaries for each tenure number in BC Mineral Titles Online before relying on the map. Tenure status can change daily.

How current is the BC data?

Each search queries the province's public mineral tenure layer, published through DataBC, at the moment you search, so it is as current as that layer. The province does not document how often the layer is refreshed from MTO, so check anything very recent in MTO.

If you want to keep watching good-to dates after the map is built, Tenure Monitor saves a claim portfolio and emails you before each deadline. It reads a synchronized copy of the same public dataset and points you back to MTO; it is not the registry.

Data source & disclaimer
Some jurisdictions are queried live; others use refreshed public datasets. BC tenure information originates with the Province of British Columbia's Mineral Titles Online and is published by DataBC. What the app can and can’t verify

Can and cannot verify
Can: show tenure geometry and attributes from public data, let you style and label them, and combine them with your own files.
Cannot: confirm legal ownership, good-standing, work-requirement compliance, or that boundaries are survey-accurate. Treat the map as a visualization, and confirm specifics in MTO.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I look up a BC mineral claim by tenure number?
Choose Claim number(s) in the search box at the top of this page, type the tenure number (for example 1044501) and press Search claims. You can paste up to 100 numbers at once. If a number is not found, the search links to Mineral Titles Online.
Can I search BC mineral claims by company name?
Yes. Choose Company and type the registered owner name. Pick the matching owner and its titles are grouped by area with claim counts, hectares and good-to dates. Titles are often held by a subsidiary, so try that name too.
Can I see who owns a BC mineral claim?
Yes. Each result shows the registered owner with its percentage ownership, the good-to date, area and title type. Confirm ownership and status in Mineral Titles Online.
How current is the BC claim data?
Each search queries the province's public tenure layer, published by DataBC, when you search, so it is as current as that layer. Tenure status can change, so verify current status and good-to dates in MTO before relying on the map.
Can I search BC claims on a map by area?
Yes. Choose Area and enter a latitude and longitude; the map opens there with every title within 25 km, and you can widen the radius to 50 or 100 km. Or leave the box empty and click a point on the map.
Is Exploration Maps the official BC claims registry?
No. BC's registry of record is Mineral Titles Online (MTO), operated by the Province of British Columbia. Use Exploration Maps to find claims and build maps, and verify ownership, status and boundaries in MTO.
What file formats can I import for BC tenures?
GeoJSON, Shapefile (.shp, zipped), and KML/KMZ. If you have corner coordinates in a spreadsheet, export it as CSV and use the Column Mapper to assign latitude and longitude. You can convert Shapefiles to GeoJSON for free at mapshaper.org.
