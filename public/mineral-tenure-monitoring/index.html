Tenure Monitor

Mineral Tenure Monitoring & Claim Expiry Reminders

Tenure Monitor watches a saved group of British Columbia mineral claims, reads their published good-to-dates, and emails you before each deadline. It also tells you when the government record for a monitored claim changes — a date that moved, an owner added or removed, a title that stopped appearing in the published dataset.

What it does

  • Saved groups of claims — a project, a region, a joint venture, or everything you hold
  • Email reminders at 90, 30 and 1 day before each good-to-date
  • Change detection on the published government record
  • A maintenance decision and note against each claim, with an audit trail
  • Claim table, portfolio map and CSV export
  • One click from any claim into the map editor

Watch ground you do not own

A monitored group is a watch list, not an ownership record. A competitor's claims, a property under evaluation, or the block next to yours can all be monitored with the same reminders as your own ground. Nothing about it is visible to the title holder.

What it deliberately does not do

It reads public data, calculates dates, sends reminders, records what you decided, and links you to MTO. The transaction stays yours.

  • It does not log into Mineral Titles Online, and never stores MTO credentials
  • It does not file assessment work or pay cash in lieu
  • It does not register, transfer or acquire title
  • It does not automatically stake released ground
  • A reminder from Exploration Maps is not a legal notice from the province

Coverage

Tenure Monitor covers British Columbia. BC publishes a good-to-date as a structured field on every title, which is what makes a reliable countdown possible; other jurisdictions express maintenance obligations differently, and a reminder built on an inferred date would sometimes be wrong about a deadline.

Claim search and mapping cover seven Canadian jurisdictions — see the Canadian registry guide.

Verify with the official registry: Government title records and Exploration Maps monitoring results should be verified in the official Mineral Titles Online registry before a transaction or deadline decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I track mineral claim expiry dates?
Save the claims you care about to a monitored group. Tenure Monitor reads each claim's published good-to-date, counts the days in Pacific time, and emails you at 90, 30 and 1 day beforehand. If the province moves a date, the reminders are recalculated and the outdated ones cancelled.
How many claims can I monitor free?
Ten, in one group, with reminders to one address at 90, 30 and 1 day before each good-to-date. Pro is $29/month and raises that to 50 claims across several groups, with a 7-day reminder and a second recipient.
Does Exploration Maps file my assessment work?
No. Tenure Monitor is read-only with respect to government transactions. It does not log into MTO, store MTO credentials, file work, pay cash in lieu or register title. You do those in Mineral Titles Online yourself; this tells you when they are due.
Can I monitor claims in provinces other than British Columbia?
Not yet. Claim search and mapping cover seven Canadian jurisdictions, but monitoring is BC-only because BC publishes a structured good-to-date on every title. Building a countdown on a date we had to infer would mean sometimes being wrong about a deadline.
Is a reminder a legal notice?
No. It is generated by Exploration Maps from public BC tenure data. It is not a notice from the province and it does not maintain, renew or register any title. Government records control, and important deadlines should be confirmed in MTO.
