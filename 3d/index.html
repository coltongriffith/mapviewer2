Exploration Maps 3D: drill hole figures for news releases and investor presentations

        
Exploration Maps 3D (beta) turns drill collars, downhole surveys and assays into a branded 3D drilling figure and a matching cross-section, in the browser, with nothing to install. The Example Ridge project on screen is a fictional sample with illustrative data.

        
What it does

        
    
          
  • Reads collars, surveys and assays as CSV or ZIP, with optional topography and OBJ model shapes, and checks them for missing collars, From ≥ To, overlapping intervals, intervals beyond hole depth, conflicting surveys, dips out of range and outlying collars.
    • 
          
  • Draws the holes in a glass or solid block with labels on its faces, over your topography, the collar elevations, or real elevation with a satellite drape once the coordinate system is set.
    • 
          
  • Colours traces by grade, with a display cut-off, intercept callouts, and "Assays pending" and provisional-position labels from the collar status.
    • 
          
  • Adds a section A–A′, and an optional grade shell interpolated from the assays at a cut-off, labelled on the figure as an exploratory envelope, not a geological model or a mineral resource.
    • 
          
  • Calculates a metal equivalent such as CuEq or AuEq from your prices and recoveries and prints the formula in the footnote.
    • 
          
  • Exports PNGs for slides, news releases, square and portrait posts and A4 print, or a layers ZIP for designers. Figures can be saved to a free Exploration Maps account or sent to the design desk.
    • 
        

        
Building and exporting figures is free. Figures show the data you load; the issuer's Qualified Person remains responsible for what a disclosure figure states.

          Metal equivalent (CuEq, AuEq…)
          
Adds a calculated column from your prices and recoveries: % → lb/t × 22.0462, g/t → oz/t ÷ 31.1035. An interval missing any metal gets no value. The formula and assumptions are added to the footnote while it is in use.

          

        

      



        
Grade shell from assays

        
        
For drilling without a model OBJ: an envelope interpolated from the assays at a cut-off. Not a geological model or a resource; the footnote says so.

        

        

          
          
          
        

        
        
        

          Advanced
          

            
            
            
            
          

        

        

          How the shell is built
          
10 m length-weighted composites down each hole, from the grade column the holes are coloured by (only assayed lengths count; a composite needs 5 m assayed). On a grid over the block (cells about 1/40 of its width, 5–25 m), each point takes the nearest composite from each hole; holes whose collars are within 20 m form one collar group and count once. The nearest groups within the search radius, at most four, are combined with inverse-distance-squared weights (5 m minimum distance), and at least three groups must be in range. The search radius is 75 m, or 1.5 times the median spacing between collar groups for wider drilling. Nothing is estimated above the terrain or collar surface. The surface is drawn by marching cubes at the cut-off and lightly smoothed.

          
No geological domains, variography, top cuts, density or economic limits are applied, and the distances are screening choices. The shell can break up or close where the drilling ends; those edges are not geological limits. No tonnage or contained metal is implied.
